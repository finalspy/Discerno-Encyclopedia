## How it works

Lifton (1961), who studied the methods of "thought reform" in Communist China, describes what he calls "loading the language": a closed vocabulary of brief, definitive and repeated formulas, deployed against any question. The formula marks the end of the discussion instead of opening it. Lifton sees this as one of the traits of ideological "totalism", which can also be found in sectarian groups and in organizations of very different kinds.

Neutral examples: "That's just how it is", "We don't debate with those people", "Everyone knows that...", "You can't understand."

## Why it is problematic

The real question is not addressed, and the person who asks it is discredited de facto. When used by a group, these formulas become markers of belonging: repeating them signals loyalty, while questioning them signals disloyalty.

## How to detect it

- A short formula is given in answer to a specific objection.
- The same sentence comes back as a reply to varied questions.
- Simply asking the question makes you suspect.

## How to protect yourself

- Rephrase the question and ask for an answer on the substance, without the formula.
- Ask what the formula concretely asserts, and what justifies it.
- Notice your own closing formulas.

## How to respond

- Do not let yourself be cornered: calmly return to the original question.
- Ask for a definition or a concrete example.
- If no answer can be obtained, note it without treating it as proof of bad faith.
