## How it works

"Some say", "numerous studies", "up to 50%": these phrases seem to inform without specifying anything. Channell (1994) shows that vague language also has ordinary and legitimate uses. It becomes a technique when it masks the absence of evidence or of a position.

## Why it is problematic

A vague claim cannot be refuted, and its author can always say they meant something else.

## How to detect it

- Unnamed sources, quantities without figures.
- Phrasing designed to please audiences with opposing views.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask who, how many, where, when.
- Ask what would make the sentence false.
