## How it works

This is the negative side of the halo effect: a salient flaw, such as clumsiness, clothing, an accent or a past mistake, leads to harsher judgments of traits that have nothing to do with it. Thorndike (1920) already described ratings that were too correlated with one another, in either direction.

In the experiment by Nisbett and Wilson (1977), the group that saw the teacher presented as distant also rated his appearance, mannerisms and accent as more irritating than the other group did, although they were identical in both versions.

## Why it is problematic

A single negative characteristic is enough to discredit a person and everything they say, including accurate statements. Personal attacks and unflattering associations exploit this mechanism to avoid discussing the arguments.

## How to detect it

- A flaw unrelated to the subject is put forward to judge the person as a whole.
- A past mistake is used to dismiss a present claim, without examining it.
- An unfavorable first impression steers the evaluation of everything else.

## How to protect yourself

- Assess each claim separately from the person who makes it.
- Ask yourself: "Does this flaw have anything to do with the question at hand?"
- List what the person is competent at before drawing a conclusion.
