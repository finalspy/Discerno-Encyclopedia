## How it works

Nisbett and Wilson (1977) argue that people have limited access to the causes of their judgments. Johansson and his colleagues (2005) replace the photo chosen by participants with another one: many do not notice and justify a choice they did not make. In neuropsychology, the word also designates a clinical disorder, distinct from this everyday bias.

## Why it is problematic

A sincere explanation is not necessarily accurate, and accounts of "why I changed my mind" should be read with caution.

## How to detect it

- A clear explanation appears after the fact.
- A preference is justified without having been examined.

## How to protect yourself

- Compare what people do with what they say.
- Keep a log of your reasons before deciding.
