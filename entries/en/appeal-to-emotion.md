## How it works

Emotion is not a fault in itself: Walton (1992) points out that it can have its place in an argument. The fallacy appears when it replaces the evidence, or when it has nothing to do with the question at hand.

## Why it is problematic

A strong emotion shortens the time given to checking and can make a weak conclusion look like a moral certainty.

## How to detect it

- Pity, anger or pride are called upon, with no data on the question.
- Doubting the conclusion is presented as a moral failing.

## How to protect yourself

- Identify the emotion being targeted, then ask which facts remain once it is taken away.
- Separate what is moving from what is established.
