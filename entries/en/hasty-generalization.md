## How it works

A few striking examples are often enough to draw a conclusion about an entire set. Hamill, Wilson and Nisbett (1980) show that participants generalize from a very striking case, even when they are warned that it is atypical.

## Why it is problematic

A real case can become a false rule about a profession, a country or a group of people.

## How to detect it

- "I know someone who...", followed by a general conclusion.
- No figures are given for the whole set concerned.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask how many cases were observed, and how they were chosen.
- Look for counterexamples and aggregate data.
