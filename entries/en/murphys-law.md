## Definition

This saying is often attributed to an American engineer of the 1940s. Spark (2006) traces its history. It can usefully remind one to plan for failures.

Status: a humorous saying, not a scientific law.

## Competing definitions and debates

As a claim about the world, it is false: failures are noticed and recounted more than everything that goes well. This can be partly explained by availability and negativity.

## Why it helps to discern

Distinguishing it from a law prevents confusing a rule of caution with a verified observation, and reminds one to count the cases where everything went well.
