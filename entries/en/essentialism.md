## How it works

Gelman (2003) shows that this way of reasoning appears in childhood. Haslam and colleagues (2000) describe how it applies to social categories, which are considered natural, stable and with clear boundaries.

## Why it is problematic

A social group then seems defined by a nature, rather than by a history and by circumstances that change.

## How to detect it

- "It's in their nature", "they are made that way".
- Clear boundaries, with no internal variation or evolution.

## How to protect yourself

- Look for variation within the group and its evolution over time.
- Ask what evidence supports the idea of a nature.
