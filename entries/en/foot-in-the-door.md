## How it works

One starts with a minimal request, easy to accept, and then makes a larger one. A person who has accepted the first is more likely to accept the second, out of consistency with their past behavior.

Freedman and Fraser (1966) observed this with homeowners asked to install a large road-safety sign in their garden: those who had first accepted a very modest request agreed far more often than the control group. Cialdini (1984) links this phenomenon to the principle of commitment and consistency.

## Why it is problematic

A progressive request is not manipulative in itself. It becomes so when the first step is designed to create a commitment whose stakes are not announced, and the person is then led to stay "consistent" rather than deciding with full knowledge.

## How to detect it

- A very light request (signing, clicking, answering a question) precedes a much more costly request (donation, subscription, data sharing).
- The next request refers to what one has already accepted ("you have already supported us").
- The real total cost is presented only at the last step.

## How to protect yourself

- Judge each request independently of the previous one: "Would I accept this if it were asked first?"
- Remember that accepting a small thing creates no obligation to accept the next.
- Ask for the total cost before accepting the first step.
