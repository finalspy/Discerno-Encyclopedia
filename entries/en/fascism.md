## Definition

The word first denoted the Italian regime and movement of the interwar period, then, depending on the authors, other movements of the same period, including German Nazism. There is no single definition. Paxton (2004) sees in it a form of political behavior marked by an obsession with decline, humiliation and the victimhood of a community, and by compensatory cults of unity, energy and purity, in which a mass party of nationalist militants, in collaboration with the traditional elites, abandons democratic freedoms and pursues, through a supposedly redemptive violence, an internal "cleansing" and external expansion.

## Competing definitions and debates

Griffin (1991) proposes a "fascist minimum", a populist ultranationalism of regeneration ("palingenetic"), whereas Payne (1995) draws up a typology of characteristics, goals and methods, and Passmore (2002) presents the debates among historians. Eco (1995) does not define fascism but lists fourteen typical traits of an "eternal fascism", including the cult of tradition, the rejection of disagreement, the fear of difference and the obsession with conspiracy; he specifies that no single trait is enough, and that a single one can make fascism "coalesce".

Paxton insists on a methodological point: fascism is understood through its stages (birth of movements, rooting in the political system, seizure of power, exercise of power, radicalization or stagnation), and it does not reproduce itself in the same form. Historians also compare fascist regimes with other twentieth-century regimes, notably the Stalinist Soviet regime. This comparison, often made under the term totalitarianism, remains debated (Fitzpatrick and Geyer, 2009).

Using the word for present-day actors is a disputed question, covered in the entry on label misuse.

## Why it helps to discern

Knowing the available definitions prevents turning the word into an insult or a mere synonym for authoritarianism. One can identify *traits* (dehumanization of a group, cult of the leader, rejection of disagreement, appeal to violence) without having to decide whether an actor "is fascist". This is the method adopted by the encyclopedia: describing signs and their degrees, not attaching labels.
