## Definition

Diaconis and Mosteller (1989) formulate it while studying coincidences: with a large enough sample, any astonishing thing has a good chance of happening. A chance of one in a million per day, applied to millions of people, produces several cases every day.

Status: a consequence of probability, not an empirical observation. It should not be confused with the law of large numbers, a different theorem.

## Competing definitions and debates

Diaconis and Mosteller (1989) place this law among several sources of coincidences, alongside hidden causes and errors in judging probability, for example when one does not count all the ways in which a coincidence could have occurred.

## Why it helps to discern

A striking coincidence is not in itself a sign of a hidden cause or a conspiracy. The question to ask is how many opportunities there were for it to occur.
