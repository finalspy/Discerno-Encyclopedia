## How it works

Forer (1949) gave his students a personality description supposed to be theirs alone. In fact they all had the same text, made up of general statements. On average they rated it as very accurate for themselves. Meehl (1956) named the phenomenon the "Barnum effect".

Vague, flattering or double-edged statements are accepted more readily, because we project our own experiences onto them and retain the passages that seem right.

## Why it is problematic

It gives the impression that a practice (horoscope, personality test without validation, fortune-telling, certain "analyses") works, when the perceived precision comes from the wording and not from real information.

## How to detect it

- Sentences such as "you sometimes have doubts about yourself but you know how to adapt".
- No precise, dated or verifiable claim.
- The text fits almost anyone, or states a trait and its opposite.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask: "Would this description fit anyone?"
- Compare with another person's description: if they are interchangeable, the effect is at work.
- Ask for a precise, testable prediction, and write it down before you know the outcome.
