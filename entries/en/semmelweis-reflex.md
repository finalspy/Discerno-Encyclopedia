## How it works

The expression comes from Semmelweis, a 19th-century physician: he showed that handwashing reduced deaths after childbirth, and his idea was long poorly received. The shortcut should not lead one to believe that every rejection hides a misunderstood genius: most rejected theories were rejected for good reasons.

## Why it is problematic

Automatic rejection deprives us of useful corrections. Conversely, invoking Semmelweis to defend an idea without evidence is another misuse.

## How to detect it

- The criticism targets what the idea upsets, not its data.
- "Semmelweis was rejected too" is offered as proof.

## How to protect yourself

- Examine the data, whatever the origin of the idea.
- Being rejected does not prove that you are right.
