## Definition

The adage is named after the British journalist who wrote it in 2009: a headline in the form of a question allows an idea to be put forward that the article cannot assert, for lack of evidence. The honest answer is then often "no".

Status: a journalistic adage, not a verified rule.

## Competing definitions and debates

Cook and Plourde (2016) tested it on titles of scientific articles, where it does not hold: the questions in these titles do not mostly call for the answer no. The adage mainly targets press usage, in which the question serves to suggest without asserting.

## Why it helps to discern

A question in a headline can plant doubt without taking responsibility for it. Ask what the article actually establishes, and whether the headline would assert the same thing without the question mark.
