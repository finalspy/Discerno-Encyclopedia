## How it works

Bad news, criticism or danger holds attention more than a positive event of the same importance. Baumeister and colleagues (2001) conclude from their review of the literature that negative events, emotions and feedback generally have a stronger and more lasting effect than their positive equivalents.

Rozin and Royzman (2001) distinguish several components, including "dominance": when positive and negative elements are combined, the overall judgment often tilts more toward the negative than a simple addition would suggest.

## Why it is problematic

Alarming content is noticed and remembered better than measured content, whatever its accuracy. A selection of negative facts can thus give a darker picture of a situation than the full data, without containing a single false piece of information.

## How to detect it

- A text lists only failures, threats or scandals, with no order of magnitude or point of comparison.
- An isolated negative case takes up more room than the general trend.
- A single criticism among many favorable reviews erases everything else from the memory one keeps of it.

## How to protect yourself

- Look for the trend over time and the total number of cases.
- Ask what a complete account would add, including what goes well.
- Wait before sharing content that worries or outrages.
