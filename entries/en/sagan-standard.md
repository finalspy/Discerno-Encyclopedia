## Definition

The formula is attached to the astronomer Carl Sagan, who popularized it around 1980, but the idea is older. Deming (2016) traces its history and links it to probabilistic reasoning: a claim that is very improbable at the outset requires more evidence to become credible.

Status: an epistemological principle, not an observation.

## Competing definitions and debates

What is "extraordinary" depends on what is already known, and this judgment can itself be biased. Used without discernment, the principle can serve to dismiss new but well-established results. Deming (2016) discusses these objections.

## Why it helps to discern

It provides a yardstick: a claim that would overturn solid knowledge cannot rest on a testimonial, a video or an isolated study.
