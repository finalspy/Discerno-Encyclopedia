## How it works

Pronin, Lin and Ross (2002) describe biases to participants and ask them how prone they are to them, themselves and others. On average, participants consider themselves less affected than others.

Scopelliti and her colleagues (2015) propose a measure of this tendency and observe differences between individuals. The impression of seeing oneself clearly is therefore not enough to guarantee a judgment free of bias.

## Why it is problematic

Someone who believes they are objective may attribute every disagreement to the other person's biases. Knowing the list of biases can even be used to discredit others rather than to question oneself: this encyclopedia is not immune to that risk of use.

## How to detect it

- The name of a bias is used to discredit an opponent, without examining their arguments.
- One thinks "I am not swayed by this".
- Each side explains the other's position by biases, and its own by the facts.

## How to protect yourself

- Apply to yourself first the criteria you apply to others.
- Identify what would change your mind, and write it down before reading the contrary view.
- Ask a person who disagrees to re-read their reasoning.
- Answer the argument before discussing the supposed bias of its author.
