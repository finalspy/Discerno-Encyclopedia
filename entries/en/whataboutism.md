## How it works

A criticism or accusation is dismissed with a question or remark of the form "What about X?", which shifts the discussion to another subject, often a reproach aimed at the person making the criticism or at their side. The original point is neither disputed nor explained.

The technique is close to *tu quoque* ("you too"), which Walton (1998) analyzes among *ad hominem* arguments: it diverts attention from the substance toward the person or toward another case.

The word itself is entered in the *Oxford English Dictionary*, and its use in public debate has been studied as a rhetorical device (Dykstra, 2020).

Neutral example: "This brand lied about the composition of its product." Reply: "And the other brands, they've never lied, perhaps?"

## Why it is problematic

The original question remains unanswered, and the discussion bogs down in an exchange of reproaches. The behavior denounced is no less problematic because another behavior is problematic too.

## How to detect it

- The reply contains nothing about the fact denounced, only another case.
- The comparison concerns a different subject, a different period or a different actor.
- It is used instead of an answer, not in addition to one.

Do not confuse it with a relevant comparison: asking for the same principle to be applied to similar cases is legitimate when the comparison concerns the same subject and supplements the answer.

## How to protect yourself

- Return to the question asked: "That does not answer this criticism."
- Treat the two subjects separately: the second may deserve discussion, but afterwards.
- Ask whether the comparison really concerns the same subject.
