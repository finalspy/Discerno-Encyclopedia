## How it works

The choice is posed as a strict alternative ("either A or B"), and the other possibilities are excluded: intermediate positions, other solutions, or the possibility of choosing neither. Refusing one option is then presented as endorsing the other.

Neutral example: "Either you come to my party on Saturday, or you are not my friend."

## Why it is problematic

The interlocutor is locked into a choice they did not formulate, and the option one wants them to accept appears to be the only reasonable one. Nuanced positions disappear from the discussion.

## How to detect it

- Phrases such as "either… or…", "there are only two possibilities", "whoever is not with us is against us".
- One of the two options is caricatured or plainly unacceptable.
- No other solution is mentioned, nor even mentioned as ruled out.

## How to protect yourself

- Look for a third option: combine the two, choose a compromise, or reject the framing.
- Ask who defined the options and why these two were chosen.
- Ask whether the options are truly mutually exclusive.
