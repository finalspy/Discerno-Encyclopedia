## Definition

This entry is a reading aid, not a rating tool. It cross-references two axes: the *intensity* of signs (from hostile rhetoric to calls for violence) and the *real power of coercion* of the person who carries them (from the individual with no means to the state apparatus). Aggressive speech without power does not carry the same weight as a measure taken by a power in office.

The models in the literature concern movements and regimes. Paxton (2004) describes five stages for fascist movements. Linz (1978) distinguishes loyal, semi-loyal and disloyal opposition with respect to democratic rules. Levitsky and Ziblatt (2018) retain four indicators of authoritarian behavior: rejection of democratic rules, denial of the legitimacy of opponents, toleration or encouragement of violence, and willingness to curtail the freedoms of opponents, media included. Bermeo (2016) and Lührmann and Lindberg (2019) show that the current democratic decline is more often gradual and legal in appearance than brutal.

*Editorial synthesis* (not the work of a single author): a drift can be read in four increasingly coercive levels: tone (hostile rhetoric), social pressure (ostracism, pressure on individuals), pressure on institutions (justice, press, administration, electoral rules), then legal or violent coercion.

## Competing definitions and debates

The democracy quality indices (V-Dem, Freedom House, Polity) rest on different definitions and measurement methods, each with distinct strengths and weaknesses (Boese, 2019). Applying these models to statements or to individuals goes beyond their purpose: they describe regimes and movements, with their acts. They are also used for polemical purposes, and the risk of false positives is real.

## Why it helps to discern

An isolated sign is not a diagnosis: what matters is the convergence of several signs, their rising intensity and the power of those who carry them. The grid helps avoid two errors: minimizing everything ("these are just words") or dramatizing everything ("it is already fascism").
