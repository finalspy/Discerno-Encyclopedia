## Definition

Stated around 1990 on Usenet forums by the American lawyer whose name it bears, and recounted by him in Godwin (1994), the law was meant to be a "counter-meme": to point out easy comparisons with Nazism so that people think twice about them, and so as not to trivialize what it was.

Status: a humorous observation, formulated as a probability but not measured.

## Competing definitions and debates

In French, "point Godwin" is often used to declare that a discussion is lost for whoever made the comparison. This use goes beyond the law, which does not say that a historical comparison is always wrong: it can be accurate and argued. Invoking Godwin's point to close an exchange then becomes a way of dismissing an argument without examining it.

## Why it helps to discern

A comparison with the worst disqualifies without demonstrating: one must ask which specific traits are being compared and whether they are established. Conversely, shouting "Godwin's point" does not replace examining the comparison.
