## How it works

Ellsberg (1961) offers a choice between two urns: the content of one is known, the other is not. Many people prefer the first, even when this leads to inconsistent choices.

## Why it is problematic

A familiar, quantified option can be preferred to one that may be better, because its uncertainty is uncomfortable. False precision can also be reassuring.

## How to detect it

- An option is ruled out only because we know less about it.
- A precise but fragile figure is preferred to an honest range.

## How to protect yourself

- Estimate a range for the uncertain option.
- Ask whether more information is really obtainable.
