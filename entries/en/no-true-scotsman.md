## How it works

Flew's (1975) classic example: "No Scotsman would do that"; a Scotsman who did it is cited; "then he is not a true Scotsman." The definition changes at the precise moment it becomes inconvenient.

## Why it is problematic

The claim becomes impossible to refute, and a group can exempt itself from anything its members do.

## How to detect it

- "A true X would never do that", after a counterexample.
- The membership criterion appears only at that moment.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask for the definition before examining the cases.
- Ask whether the same criterion would apply to another group.
