## How it works

Davison (1983) describes the tendency to think that a persuasive message has more effect on others than on oneself. Perloff (1999) reviews the subsequent work, which often finds this gap in perception, and examines what makes it vary, such as the topic or the perceived distance from the "others".

## Why it is problematic

Believing oneself protected leads to less checking of what one reads. This perception can also be accompanied by stronger support for content restrictions, justified by the protection of others rather than by a measure of the actual effect. This gap does not say who is actually influenced.

## How to detect it

- Phrases such as "I can tell what's what, but people will believe it."
- A measure is demanded in the name of an assumed influence of some content on others, with no data on that influence.
- The public is described as credulous, and the author as clear-sighted.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask what would allow you to claim that others are more influenced than you are.
- Look for measures of the actual effect of content, not just impressions.
- Check content even when you feel little concerned by its influence.
