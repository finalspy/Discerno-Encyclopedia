## How it works

Baron and Hershey (1988) present participants with identical medical or gambling decisions, with different outcomes. A decision is judged better if it turned out well.

## Why it is problematic

A lucky gamble passes for genius, and a reasonable decision that turned out badly passes for a mistake. This distorts assessments and the attribution of responsibility.

## How to detect it

- Success is the only evidence given for the quality of a decision.
- A decision is criticized using information known only afterwards.

## How to protect yourself

- Judge the decision on the basis of what was known at the time.
- Also look at similar decisions that turned out badly.
