## How it works

Allport (1954) describes how frustration can be directed against a group that is not its cause. Glick (2005) shows that the choice of the targeted group is not random: in times of crisis, a group perceived as both powerful and threatening becomes an easy target, because it is attributed an intent to harm.

The method consists of designating a group as *the* one responsible for a complex situation, presenting its elimination or exclusion as the solution, and rallying the others around that designation.

## Why it is problematic

It replaces the analysis of causes with the designation of a culprit, which prevents addressing the real causes. It fuels hostility toward people for what they are or for what they are believed to be.

## How to detect it

- A complex difficulty (economic, social, health-related) is attributed to a single group.
- The group is described as a homogeneous and malicious whole ("they want", "they do it on purpose").
- The proposed solution is to remove or punish that group rather than act on causes.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask: "What other causes could explain this situation?"
- Distinguish criticism of specific behaviors from blaming an entire group.
- Replace the designated group with another in the sentence: if the argument keeps the same form, it is a technique, not an analysis.

## How to respond

- Ask for evidence of the causal link between the group and the problem.
- Offer a fuller explanation, with its sources, without in turn designating another single culprit.
- Support the people targeted and report content that calls for illegal acts.
