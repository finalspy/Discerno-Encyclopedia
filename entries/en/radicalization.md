## Definition

Radicalization refers to the shift toward a greater readiness to support or use illegitimate means, notably violence, in the service of a cause. McCauley and Moskalenko (2017) distinguish radicalization of *opinions*, which concerns what one supports, from radicalization of *action*, which concerns what one is ready to do: the great majority of people with radical opinions never move to action.

## Competing definitions and debates

Pathway models (Moghaddam's "staircase", 2005) are useful metaphors that the data poorly confirm: Borum (2011) concludes that there is no single profile or trajectory. Kruglanski and colleagues (2014) emphasize the "quest for significance": the need to matter, which a cause can satisfy.

The process does not depend on ideology: it is found in political causes on opposing sides, in religious causes, or in causes focused on a single issue. The term is also criticized for its police or political use, which can extend to opinions that are simply in the minority.

## Why it helps to discern

Distinguishing opinions from actions prevents confusing a radical idea with a danger. The signs to watch for are not the theme defended, but the narrowing of one's social world around a group, the dehumanization of the opponent, and the justification of violence.
