## Definition

Stigler, a statistician, stated it in 1980 (Stigler, 1980) and attributed it himself to the sociologist Robert K. Merton, which makes it its own illustration. A discovery is more often named after the person who made it famous than after the person who made it.

Status: an observation from the history of science, phrased humorously; "no" is an exaggeration.

## Competing definitions and debates

Several entries in this section are examples of this: Benford's law had been observed by Newcomb, and the Pareto principle owes its 80/20 form to other authors. The phenomenon connects with what Merton called the Matthew effect: credit goes to those who are already recognized.

## Why it helps to discern

The name attached to an idea or a quotation does not guarantee its origin. Famous quotations are often wrongly attributed: check the primary source before relying on a name.
