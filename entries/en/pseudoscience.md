## Definition

According to Hansson, a doctrine is pseudoscientific when it meets three conditions: it is not scientific (it does not meet the requirements of the scientific method), it deals with a domain that science studies, and it is presented as scientific or as better founded than science.

Pseudoscience is therefore not simply an error: it is a claim that borrows the prestige of science without accepting its constraints (testable hypotheses, possible refutation, correction through criticism).

## Competing definitions and debates

Defining the boundary precisely is difficult: this is the demarcation problem (see the dedicated entry). Popper (1963) proposes falsifiability; his critics note that it excludes too much or too little. Pigliucci and Boudry (2013) instead defend a body of clues to be weighed together.

Being in the minority or in disagreement with the majority is not enough: science has sometimes been wrong, and ideas first rejected have later been confirmed. What matters is how an idea defends itself (tests, data, openness to criticism), not its status at the moment.

## Why it helps to discern

A few clues, to be considered together rather than one by one: the claim cannot be refuted, or it is rescued after each failure by added explanations; it rests on testimonials rather than controlled trials; it does not progress and does not correct itself; it avoids examination by independent peers; it claims the establishment rejects it through conspiracy; it borrows scientific vocabulary without its rigor.
