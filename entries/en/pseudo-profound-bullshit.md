## How it works

Frankfurt (2005) distinguishes lying, which contradicts the truth, from *bullshit*, which stems from indifference to the truth: one seeks the effect, not accuracy.

Pennycook and colleagues (2015) had participants rate sentences randomly built from fashionable abstract words, yet grammatically correct. Many judged them profound. People who more readily think analytically were less susceptible to them.

## Why it is problematic

Empty talk is taken for wisdom or expertise, and can be used to sell a method, a book or an idea that can never be verified.

## How to detect it

- Abstract and solemn words ("energy", "consciousness", "transformation", "quantum") without definition.
- One cannot say what would be observed if the statement were false.
- Restated simply, the sentence no longer says anything precise.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask: "What does this concretely assert? How could it be verified?"
- Try to restate the sentence in simple terms; if this fails, the depth is probably only apparent.
- Be wary of a discourse whose obscurity is presented as a sign of depth.
