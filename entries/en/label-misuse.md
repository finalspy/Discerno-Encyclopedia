## Definition

Sartori (1970) describes "conceptual stretching": the more a concept is applied to many cases, the less precise content it has, until it no longer distinguishes anything. A loaded label used in place of an argument serves as an insult: it designates the person or group as unworthy of discussion. Walton (1998) classes this procedure among *ad hominem* arguments.

Godwin's law (1994) observes that the longer an online discussion goes on, the more likely a comparison with Nazism becomes. Its author specified that the point was not to forbid relevant comparisons.

## Competing definitions and debates

Where does the abuse begin? Specialists themselves differ on the criteria for words such as "fascism" or "totalitarianism". A serious historical comparison, which spells out its criteria and limits, is not an abuse; using the word as a conclusion, without criteria, is.

The procedure belongs to no camp: every political orientation has words that serve to disqualify ("fascist", "communist", "conspiracy theorist", "extremist", "pseudoscientific"...).

## Why it helps to discern

Faced with a label, two questions can be asked: *What precise criteria does this word cover?* and *Which of them are met, and with what evidence?* A label without criteria is not information. Conversely, refusing any use of a historical word for facts that clearly meet its criteria is also a way of closing the discussion.
