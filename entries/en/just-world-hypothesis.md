## How it works

Lerner and Simmons (1966) have participants observe a person who seems to receive electric shocks. When they cannot do anything for her, some devalue the victim rather than admit that she suffers for no reason.

According to Lerner and Miller's (1978) interpretation, believing that the world is just is reassuring: if everyone gets what they deserve, one can feel protected as long as one acts "well". A manifest injustice threatens this belief, which can then be preserved by looking for a fault in the person affected.

## Why it is problematic

This belief can turn a victim into someone responsible and make misfortune or success appear to be proof of personal worth. It diverts attention from the real causes of a situation and can serve to close the discussion without examining the facts.

## How to detect it

- Phrases such as "he had it coming", "there's no smoke without fire" or "everyone gets what they deserve", with no factual element.
- Success or failure alone is used to judge a person's worth.
- The victim's behavior is examined before, and instead of, that of the perpetrator.

## How to protect yourself

- Separate two questions: what happened, and what the person deserved.
- Take chance and circumstances into account in what happens to people.
- Notice your need to find a fault after an event that worries you.
