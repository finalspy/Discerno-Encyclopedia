## How it works

No scientific knowledge is one hundred percent certain. The technique consists of insisting on this residual uncertainty, funding or promoting voices that stress it, and calling for "more research" before any decision, so as to delay a regulation or a change that threatens an interest.

Internal documents from the tobacco industry, submitted in American legal proceedings, show a strategy of this type (Michaels, 2008; Oreskes and Conway, 2010). The authors of *Agnotology* (Proctor and Schiebinger, 2008) speak of the production of ignorance.

The technique does not depend on a camp: any actor with an interest in delaying a decision can use it, on any subject.

## Why it is problematic

A minor uncertainty is presented as a fundamental disagreement. The decision is postponed and the exposed population remains at risk for longer.

## How to detect it

- "Science is not settled", without saying on what precisely or by how much.
- Experts put forward without their funding or conflicts of interest being disclosed.
- The demand for absolute proof before acting, although the decision concerns probabilities.

## How to protect yourself

- Distinguish the existence of an uncertainty from its extent: does it concern the essentials or details?
- Look for the position of syntheses and scientific academies, and check the funding of the voices cited.
- Ask what waiting would cost, and for whom.
