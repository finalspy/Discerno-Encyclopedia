## Definition

Formulated in 2013 by the Italian programmer whose name it bears, the law states that the energy needed to refute nonsense is an order of magnitude greater than the energy needed to produce it. Williamson (2016) takes it up to call on scientists to correct false information nonetheless.

Status: an informal observation, not a measured law. The "order of magnitude" ratio is an image, not a result.

## Competing definitions and debates

The asymmetry has known causes: a claim fits in a sentence, while verifying it requires sources, context and time, and false information goes on influencing people after it has been corrected. What remains debated is the conclusion to draw: correcting systematically, as Williamson (2016) proposes, or choosing one's battles.

## Why it helps to discern

It explains why an avalanche of poorly supported claims can seem unanswered without being right. A challenger's silence proves nothing: they may simply lack time.
