## How it works

Rosenthal and Fode (1963) told students that their rats were "bright" or "slow", although the rats had been assigned at random. The results followed these announcements. Blind evaluation is used to limit this effect.

## Why it is problematic

A result can reflect what one was looking for, in good faith, without anyone having cheated.

## How to detect it

- The evaluator knows which group each participant belongs to.
- The study does not mention blinding.

## How to protect yourself

- Check whether the study was conducted double-blind.
- Prefer results reproduced by independent teams.
