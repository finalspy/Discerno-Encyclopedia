## Definition

The demarcation problem is the question of which criteria distinguish science from non-science, and in particular from pseudoscience. Popper (1959) proposes falsifiability: a scientific theory states what, if observed, would contradict it.

## Competing definitions and debates

No single criterion has won agreement. Kuhn (1962) stresses the role of communities and paradigms; Lakatos (1970) proposes evaluating research programs over time; Laudan (1983) argues that the problem is ill-posed and that it is better to assess precise claims than to classify disciplines. Pigliucci and Boudry (2013) defend a set of indicators rather than a sharp boundary.

This basic disagreement does not mean that "everything is equally valid": one can find a claim poorly founded without having a perfect definition of science.

## Why it helps to discern

It avoids two errors: believing that a "scientific" label is enough to guarantee a claim, and believing that without a perfect criterion nothing can be judged. Instead, one assesses precise signs (testability, evidence, response to criticism, correction of errors).
