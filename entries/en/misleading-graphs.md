## How it works

An axis that does not start at zero, a scale changed midway, a chosen period, disproportionate areas: the figure is right, the impression is not. Huff (1954) and Tufte (1983) describe these distortions.

## Why it is problematic

A glance is enough to form an opinion, and few people read the axes. A truncated axis can sometimes be justified, provided it is flagged.

## How to detect it

- The vertical axis does not start at zero, or it is not labeled.
- The period shown starts or stops at a convenient moment.
- The drawn areas are not proportional to the values.

## How to protect yourself

- Read the axes and the period before the curve.
- Find the source and redraw the graph if possible.
