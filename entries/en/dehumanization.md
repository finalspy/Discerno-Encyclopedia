## How it works

Haslam (2006) describes two forms: denying others the traits that are proper to humans (culture, reason, civility), or likening them to machines or objects. Animal images ("vermin", "parasites", "pests") are a common version. Bandura (1999) shows that it is part of the mechanisms of "moral disengagement": one stops feeling responsible toward those one no longer sees as fellow humans.

A comparative study of the propaganda of regimes with opposing ideologies (Bytwerk, 2004) shows the use of procedures of this kind in opposing camps.

## Why it is problematic

It prepares violence and discrimination by withdrawing in advance the compassion owed to people. It also makes disagreement impossible: one does not argue with a "parasite".

## How to detect it

- Terms that compare a group of people to harmful animals, diseases or waste.
- A group is described as lacking reason, feelings or moral worth.
- The discourse addresses "them" as a problem to be eliminated, not as people to be persuaded.

## How to protect yourself

- Replace the group with another in the sentence: if it becomes shocking, the technique is at work.
- Ask what kind of conduct one is invited to adopt toward these people.
- Remember that criticizing an idea, an act or a power does not require denying the humanity of those who hold them.

## How to respond

- Do not pass the content on, or quote it only to criticize it, without repeating it.
- Ask the person what they mean, without adopting the same register.
- Report the content to the platform or, if it is illegal, to the competent authorities.
- Support the people targeted. Avoid dehumanizing in return: that feeds the same mechanism.
