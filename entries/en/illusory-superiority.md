## How it works

In Svenson's (1981) study, a majority of drivers rated themselves as safer and more skillful than the average driver. Alicke (1985) observes that this gap concerns mainly flattering traits, and traits that people believe they can control.

## Why it is problematic

Flattering discourse, of the kind "you are not fooled", exploits this tendency and makes it harder to recognize one's mistakes.

## How to detect it

- A message flatters its audience's intelligence in order to win them over.
- Self-assessment is made without any external measure.

## How to protect yourself

- Look for an objective measure of your performance.
- Be wary of messages that open with compliments.
