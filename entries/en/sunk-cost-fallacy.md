## How it works

A rational decision should depend only on future costs and benefits. In practice, what has already been spent weighs on the choice: giving up would feel like having "lost" the investment.

Arkes and Blumer (1985) showed that theatergoers who had paid full price for their subscription attended performances more often than those who had obtained it at a reduced price, although the sum already paid could not be recovered in any case.

## Why it is problematic

It pushes people to persist in projects, purchases or relationships whose continuation is unfavorable. It can also be exploited: reminding someone of what they have already given encourages them to give more.

## How to detect it

- The main argument for continuing is "we have already invested so much."
- A solicitation relies on what the person has already paid or endured to obtain more.
- No assessment of future costs and benefits is presented.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask: "If I were starting from zero today, would I make this choice?"
- Write down separately what is lost regardless and what still depends on the decision.
- Set a stopping criterion in advance.
