## How it works

Tversky and Koehler (1994) show that an event described in detail seems more probable than the same event described globally. Itemizing the causes of death thus raises their estimated total.

## Why it is problematic

A detailed list of risks can inflate their perceived probability, without any additional information.

## How to detect it

- One risk is broken down into many cases, while another is given as a single block.
- The sum of the estimated percentages exceeds 100%.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask for the overall figure.
- Compare categories presented in the same way.
