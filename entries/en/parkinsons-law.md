## Definition

British historian C. Northcote Parkinson published it in 1955 in a satirical essay in the weekly The Economist, later reprinted in a book (Parkinson, 1957). The essay mainly targets administrations, whose staff numbers would grow independently of the amount of work to be done.

Status: a satire, not an established law. The same book contains the law of futility, which has its own entry.

## Competing definitions and debates

The sentence has become organizational advice (set short deadlines), which was not the purpose of the essay. The figures the essay puts forward on administrations served the irony and do not constitute proof.

## Why it helps to discern

It reminds us that a famous adage can come from a humorous text: its fame says nothing about its truth. Before relying on a "law", look for where it comes from and what supports it.
