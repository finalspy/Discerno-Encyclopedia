## How it works

Weber (1922) describes charismatic authority, based on belief in a person's exceptional qualities. The cult of the leader is an organized version of it: images, stories and rites surround a leader endowed with extraordinary qualities and infallibility. Kershaw (1987) studied the "myth" built around the German leader of the 1930s, and Plamper (2012) the Soviet cult of Stalin: two regimes with opposing ideologies that produced a comparable cult through similar propaganda means.

## Why it is problematic

When criticism of the leader becomes treason, disagreement disappears and errors are no longer corrected. The cult replaces rules and institutions with loyalty to a person.

## How to detect it

- A leader is presented as the only one able to save the group, or as infallible.
- Criticism of the person is treated as an attack on the cause.
- Images, slogans and rituals focus on the leader's name or face.

## How to protect yourself

- Separate what a person proposes from what they represent symbolically.
- Ask which criticisms would be permitted, and what it would cost to voice them.
- Apply the same scrutiny to every leader, whatever their side.

## How to respond

- Defend the right to criticize, aiming at decisions rather than at the person.
- Ask for precise accountability for actions, rather than taking a stance of adoration or rejection.
- Support the institutions and counterweights (press, courts, assemblies) that allow errors to be corrected.
