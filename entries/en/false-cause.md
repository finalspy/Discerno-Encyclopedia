## How it works

Two facts that follow each other or evolve together are not necessarily linked by a cause. The link may come from a third factor, from reverse causation or from chance. Holland (1986) recalls that an observed association is not enough to establish a causal effect.

## Why it is problematic

A false cause leads to bad decisions, and allows a success or a failure to be attributed to whatever one wants to promote or accuse.

## How to detect it

- "Since X, we have seen Y", with nothing else.
- A single cause is given for a complex phenomenon.
- No comparison group is mentioned.

## How to protect yourself

- Look for a third factor and for the reverse explanation.
- Ask what happened where X did not occur.
- Prefer studies designed to test a cause.
