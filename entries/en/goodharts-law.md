## Definition

Goodhart, an economist, stated it in 1975 about monetary policy: an observed statistical regularity tends to disappear as soon as one relies on it to steer. His text is reprinted in Goodhart (1984). The commonly used formulation is that of the anthropologist Strathern (1997), concerning the evaluation of universities.

Status: an empirical observation, supported by many cases in economics and management, but not a law in the strict sense.

## Competing definitions and debates

People being evaluated adapt their behavior to the indicator rather than to what it was supposed to measure. The law does not say that indicators must be abandoned, but that an indicator under pressure must be cross-checked with others and revised. Campbell's law describes the same phenomenon in public policy.

## Why it helps to discern

A figure that improves does not necessarily mean the situation is improving. When a result is presented through a single indicator, ask whether that indicator was also the target to be reached.
