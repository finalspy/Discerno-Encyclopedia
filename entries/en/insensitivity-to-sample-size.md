## How it works

In a problem by Tversky and Kahneman (1974), participants are asked which hospital, small or large, records more days on which more than 60% of births are boys. Many answer that it is the same, although the small hospital deviates more from the average.

## Why it is problematic

A spectacular result often comes from a small sample, precisely because small samples produce more extreme values.

## How to detect it

- A percentage is given without the number of cases.
- The extremes of a ranking are small entities.

## How to protect yourself

- Always ask for the number of cases, and the margin of error.
- Be wary of impressive percentages computed on few people.
