## How it works

When we have to estimate a quantity (a price, a duration, a percentage), we often start from a reference value, the anchor, then adjust. The adjustment is usually insufficient: the final estimate stays close to the anchor, even when the anchor has nothing to do with the question.

In the classic experiment by Tversky and Kahneman (1974), a rigged wheel stopped on a number at random before participants estimated the percentage of African countries in the UN. Groups whose wheel had stopped on 10 gave a median estimate of 25%, those whose wheel had stopped on 65 a median of 45%.

## Why it is problematic

The first figure quoted can decide the outcome of a negotiation, a judgment or a purchase without the person noticing. A figure chosen for its power to anchor, rather than for its information value, distorts the comparison that follows.

## How to detect it

- A high or very low figure is placed first (headline, first offer, "before" price) ahead of the value actually under discussion.
- The anchor value comes with no source and no calculation method.
- The comparison offered has a single reference point.

## How to protect yourself

- Write down your own estimate before looking at the figure offered.
- Look for at least two independent references for the same quantity.
- Ask: "Would I have estimated the same thing without seeing this figure?"
