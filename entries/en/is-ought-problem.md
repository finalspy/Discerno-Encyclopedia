## Definition

The philosopher David Hume remarks, in Book III of his Treatise (Hume, 1739; this book was published in 1740), that many arguments pass without justification from propositions about what is to propositions about what ought to be. A moral or political conclusion requires at least one premise about what is desirable; facts alone are not enough.

Status: a principle of logic and moral philosophy, discussed by philosophers.

## Competing definitions and debates

Searle (1964) argued that one can derive an "ought" from an "is" from institutional facts such as a promise; his demonstration is itself contested. The principle remains commonly used to identify the hidden value premise in an argument.

## Why it helps to discern

"It is natural," "it has always been this way" or "it is new" describe a fact, not a reason to approve of it. Faced with a conclusion about what should be done, look for the value it rests on and discuss it openly.
