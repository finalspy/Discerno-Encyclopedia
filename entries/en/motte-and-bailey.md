## How it works

Shackel (2005) takes up the image of a medieval fortress: a bailey that is pleasant but hard to defend, and a motte, the castle mound that is easy to hold. The ambitious thesis is advanced as long as it is not contested; once contested, it is replaced by an obvious point that no one denies.

## Why it is problematic

The opponent seems to deny an obvious point, while the strong thesis stays in circulation without having been defended.

## How to detect it

- A strong claim turns, under criticism, into "I'm just saying that...".
- The same word changes meaning during the exchange.

## How to protect yourself

- Have the exact thesis specified before responding.
- Separate the two versions: "On this one we agree, and on the other?"
