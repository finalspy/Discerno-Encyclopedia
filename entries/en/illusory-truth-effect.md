## How it works

Familiarity is confused with truth: what one has already heard is processed more easily by the mind, and this ease is interpreted as a sign of accuracy.

Hasher, Goldstein and Toppino (1977) presented participants with a series of statements, some true and others false, then repeated the exercise with some of the same statements. Statements already seen were judged truer than a new statement, whether true or false.

## Why it is problematic

Repeating a statement is enough to make it more credible, without any new argument or evidence. This makes slogans, rumors and the mass dissemination of one message effective.

## How to detect it

- The same message comes back in nearly identical forms, across several channels or through several accounts.
- A slogan replaces the argument and is reused as is.
- The claim "seems familiar", but one no longer knows where the information came from.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask where the information comes from, not just whether it seems familiar.
- Find the primary source before passing a claim on.
- Remember that hearing something often adds no evidence.
