## How it works

Samuelson and Zeckhauser (1988) show that an option presented as the current situation is chosen more often than the same option presented as an alternative. Loss aversion and the cost of deciding may contribute to this.

## Why it is problematic

The default setting influences the outcome without ever being discussed, and a situation can persist through inertia rather than through choice.

## How to detect it

- The default option is not justified.
- Only the risks of change are assessed, not those of inaction.

## How to protect yourself

- Compare all options, including the current one, on the same criteria.
- Ask whether you would choose it if you were starting from zero.
