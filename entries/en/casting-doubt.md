## How it works

The technique asserts nothing verifiable: it asks questions and speaks of "grey areas". Oreskes and Conway (2010) document historical campaigns in which sustaining doubt was enough to delay a decision. Doubt remains at the heart of critical thinking when it rests on evidence and accepts answers.

## Why it is problematic

Insinuation leaves a trace even without proof, and its author can always deny having accused anyone.

## How to detect it

- "We can still ask ourselves…", without any element.
- The answers given do not close the questions.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask which specific fact underpins the suspicion.
- Ask what answer would satisfy the person casting doubt.
