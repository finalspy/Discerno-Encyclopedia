## How it works

A body of data or studies contains varied results. Only those that support the thesis are cited, or the period, scale or comparison that produces the desired effect is chosen. Diethelm and McKee (2009) classify this selectivity among the techniques of denial: relying on an isolated article that contradicts the consensus while ignoring the whole body of results.

The technique works without any falsehood: each figure cited may be accurate; it is the whole that is misleading.

## Why it is problematic

The overall impression does not match the actual state of knowledge. The reader cannot see what has been set aside.

## How to detect it

- A single study or a few anecdotes are put forward against a body of work.
- The period or scale of a chart starts or stops at a convenient point.
- No mention of known contrary results, nor of how the data were selected.

## How to protect yourself

- Look for syntheses (systematic reviews, meta-analyses, academy reports) rather than isolated studies.
- Ask what has been left aside, and why this period or this comparison.
- Redo the comparison over another period or with another measure, if the data are available.
