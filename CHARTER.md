# Editorial charter

Rules every entry follows. They apply to the whole encyclopedia and are strictest for the section on authoritarian drift and obscurantism.

The encyclopedia describes **forms** (techniques, signs, mechanisms), never conclusions, people or political sides.

## Naming

1. No currently active political party or movement, no living person and no living political actor is named in any entry or example. Historical regimes and movements may be named when the entry is about them. Authors of references are cited as authors, not as examples.
2. Entries describe behaviors in texts and institutions, not identities or groups.
3. The word "fascist" and its derivatives are never applied to a current actor. "Fascism" is a historical concept described through the competing scholarly definitions.
4. Illustrative examples are either invented and neutral, or historical and documented. An entry about a political ideology uses at least two historical instances from opposed ideological camps.

## Neutrality tests

5. **Swap test.** Each indicator must read identically, and stay acceptable, when applied to any ideological camp. An entry whose definition names or implies a camp is rejected.
6. **Balanced corpus.** The entries are educational. If a scoring or an analysis were ever built on them (not planned), its method would first be published and tested on a corpus balanced across the political spectrum, with the results published.
7. **Convergence, intensity, power.** A single sign is never a diagnosis. Entries on gradation state that what matters is the convergence of several signs, their intensity, and the actual power to coerce (words in a comment thread are not state power). Entries do not create equivalence between different levels of power.
8. **Misuse.** The encyclopedia includes entries on the misuse of labels ("fascist", "conspiracist", "pseudoscientific") as insults.

## Sourcing

9. Every factual claim is supported by a listed reference: peer-reviewed work, a scholarly book, a primary document, or a recognized reference institution. Encyclopedias and press articles may be added as pointers, not as the only support.
10. A contested claim has at least two independent sources. Where scholars disagree, the entry says so and presents the positions without ranking them by political convenience.
11. References are checked against the actual work (title, authors, year, venue) by a reviewer before publication; a DOI or an Open Library record found for a reference is verified against its title, authors and year, never trusted for its title alone. Wikipedia and classification identifiers (DISARM, SemEval, LOGIC) are pointers, never proof.
12. Ideas and facts are reused; text is not, unless the source license allows it and `adapted_from` says so. CC BY-NC sources are cited only.

13. **Outbound links.** Each reference carries a stable link when one exists: the DOI (field `doi`, without the resolver prefix) for articles and many books, otherwise the page of the publisher or of an institution, the Open Library record for books, Wikisource or Gutenberg for public domain texts. Wikipedia links are built from the identifiers of `classifications.wikipedia`. No retailer or affiliate links, no link shorteners, no aggregator shortcuts. Links may only point to the hosts listed in `scripts/link-hosts.ts`, which the validator enforces; adding a host takes a pull request that justifies it. Links are checked before publication and re-checked periodically.

## Review

14. `draft` then `in-review` then `published`. Entries in the sensitive section are published only after a review by someone qualified in the field (history, political science, psychology, philosophy of science), recorded with the review date. An entry that still lists open issues (`open_issues`) is not published until each one is closed.
15. A published entry is corrected in public: the history of changes is the git history.
16. Nothing here is a verdict on a person or an organization.

## Responding

17. Advice on how to react to or counter a sign is limited to lawful, non-violent and proportionate actions: verifying, asking for sources, documenting, reporting to a platform or an authority, supporting the people targeted, taking part in civic life. It never recommends harassment, publishing private information, or dehumanizing the other side. The swap test applies to this advice as well.
